# Library, contents and everyday workflow

[Back to README](../README.md) · [Demo walkthroughs](demo.md#things-to-try)

## Pages

- **The shell** ([src/App.vue](../src/App.vue)): an icon rail on the left for the three places that are not a book — Library, Queue (running count as a badge) and Endpoints (a dot when one needs fixing); the chevron at its foot widens it to labels ([src/components/AppRail.vue](../src/components/AppRail.vue)). The open book lives in the header: its cover and title are a **book selector** ([src/components/BookSelector.vue](../src/components/BookSelector.vue)) that lists the other books with their next step and keeps you on the page you are on, with a search from six books up. Under it, on the book's own pages, a **tab row** ([src/components/BookTabs.vue](../src/components/BookTabs.vue)) — Overview, Contents, Cast, Review, Search, Scripting, Narration and Export with their counts — ends with the book's one next step as a pill. On Library, Queue and Endpoints the selector stays, dimmed, and the tab row goes. The shelf, the overview and the shell read what they say about a book from one place, `bookFacts` in [src/views/library/bookFacts.ts](../src/views/library/bookFacts.ts), through [src/composables/useShell.ts](../src/composables/useShell.ts), so the three agree.
- **Library** → **Add EPUB** → **Contents** (what goes in the audiobook) → **Book overview** (volumes, per-stage progress, cast summary, budget, what next and what else is waiting) → **Scripting**, **Narration**, **Export**.
- **Contents**: three tabs. **To decide** is an inbox of the chapters the import flagged, grouped by kind of notice; **All chapters** is every chapter in reading order, grouped by volume, to skip or restore singly, in ranges or by volume; **Volumes** is where the book is cut into volumes. See [below](#import-contents-and-the-library-shelf).
- **Review**: every decision the book is waiting on, in one list — retakes waiting for a verdict, flagged clips, speakers a re-script brought in, merge suggestions, expressions whose position needs choosing again, chunks that didn't verify, chapters the import wasn't sure about, and runs that failed ([src/views/review/inbox.ts](../src/views/review/inbox.ts)). Rows are grouped by where each is settled and link to the row itself rather than the top of its page. A batch of chapters carrying the same notice is one row, because one verdict settles them all. The page decides nothing itself; a decision settled anywhere leaves the list.
- **Cast**: every speaker across all chapters, with line and chapter counts (never which chapters, nor the model's description, which would spoil the book), gender set inline, merge suggestions for near-duplicate names, and a bar for the ticked speakers (shift-click ticks a range). Double-click a name, or press Enter on its row, to rename it; open a row for the full record. The **Character voice** row sets what a speaker with no voice of their own is read in before the Narrator's: one voice for all of them, or one each for male, female and other.
- **Search** (`/book/:id/search`, or type two or more characters in the palette): text, speaker and direction across every scripted chapter. Results deep-link to the line (`?ch=&seg=`) and can be selected for a [bulk correction](scripting.md#bulk-corrections-in-search).
- **Queue**: every job across books with cancel, retry and remove, the endpoint pool's busy slots and cooldowns, and an ETA; it can notify you when a book's run finishes. Click a job for its activity log and run details ([endpoints](endpoints.md)).
- **Endpoints**: every scripting and speech endpoint — health, throughput, spend, request history, connection, voices, limits and budgets ([endpoints](endpoints.md)).

## Stages and volumes

Library → Scripting → Narration → Export. Pick a book in Library and the other stages open for it.

A novel may span several EPUB files. Each file is a **volume**; chapters number continuously across volumes and the cast is shared. Add one with **Add a volume…** in a book's `⋯` menu or from the Add EPUB dialog. On the book overview volumes can be reordered (drag, or the ▲▼ buttons), renamed and removed. Reordering renumbers the chapters to follow the volume order. Removing a volume (a wrong EPUB) deletes its chapters, scripts, clips, jobs and export entries and renumbers the rest so numbering stays continuous; removing the only volume removes the novel. Neither removal can be undone, so both ask first.

## Chapter and line states

- **Scripting**: `done`; `fallback` — a chunk didn't verify and is kept whole as narration, to retry per chunk or split by hand from the reader; `failed` — nothing kept, try smaller chunks.
- **Narration**: `done`; `stale` — a clip no longer matches its line because the script, the dictionary, the voice or the endpoint's sample rate moved (the ledger's row details say which), and **Re-narrate changed** renders only those lines, including halves of a split line that were never rendered; `failed` — a request failed, or the chapter is only partly rendered, with a retry per line. A pause never makes anything stale: silence is stitched, not rendered.
- **Takes**: a line can hold several. `segment.audio` is always the clip in the book; a retake renders into `segment.candidate` and replaces nothing — not the player, the chapter's length or the export — until it is accepted. Rejected takes stay in the row's details ([audio review](audio.md#audio-review-and-segment-boundaries)).
- **Site text and notes**: a line of the script is story (narration, dialogue, thought), the site's text (`watermark`, never read) or a translator's or author's note (`note`, read only when the book says so). A marked line stays in the script word for word, and the detector's suggestion under a line (`siteCheck`) waits for **Accept** or **Dismiss** ([site text](scripting.md#site-text-and-translators-notes)).
- **Script versions**: a re-script, a bulk correction, a restore and each run of manual edits preserve the script they replace, and **History** in the reader previews, compares and restores one. A version is the chapter's script only, never its audio or the book's cast, dictionary or endpoints ([script history](scripting.md#chapter-script-history)).
- **Exports**: an export is one deliverable in one or more files, versioned by name, format and layout. It stores a fingerprint of every chapter it was built from, so "needs an update" is a comparison against the book rather than a date, and a failed or cancelled build never replaces the version already on disk ([exports](exports.md)).

## Voices, endpoints and spending

- **Voices belong to endpoints.** Each endpoint lists its voices (fetched from the provider, added by id, or cloned); character pickers group them by endpoint, and a paused endpoint's voices are listed but disabled. A character's voice is a ref `endpointId/voiceId`, so every line is rendered by the endpoint that owns its speaker's voice; a speaker with no voice of their own is read in the book's Character voice, or else the Narrator's, and narration is always the Narrator's. A removed voice or a paused endpoint shows as a blocker on the voice table and in the run estimate, and a line that cannot be routed fails with a reason instead of hanging.
- **Per-request character limit** (`maxChars`, 0 = whole lines). A longer line is sent as several requests and joined. **Cut at** chooses the boundary — sentence end, clause (`, ; : —`), word or hard cut — and falls back to the next finer one when the preferred boundary does not occur in the window, flagging that part ([src/lib/split.ts](../src/lib/split.ts)). The endpoint's Requests tab previews the cuts on the open book's first chapter, the run estimate counts requests, and a ledger row marks "N parts" with the exact cuts in its details.
- **Rate limits.** A 429 puts the endpoint into a cooldown on the server's speech gate, which holds every other line for that endpoint until it ends rather than sending each into the same refusal ([server/providers/gate.ts](../server/providers/gate.ts)). A paused endpoint holds its work the same way instead of failing it.
- **Keys, budgets and settings.** An API key is saved to the server and never sent back to the page ([where a key lives](backend.md#the-providers-and-where-a-key-lives)). The Endpoints page exports and imports endpoints, profiles and script settings as JSON, never keys. Each book has a budget cap and a pause for new work on its overview; what it has spent is summed from the server's append-only usage ledger, not from the clips its lines hold now ([pricing](pricing.md)).

## Shared editing and navigation

**Getting around**

- **Command palette** — `⌘K` / `Ctrl+K` or the header button ([src/components/CommandPalette.vue](../src/components/CommandPalette.vue), rows in [commands.ts](../src/components/commands.ts)): a reka-ui Dialog and Listbox filtered with `useFilter`. Jump to any page, book, chapter (opened at the stage it is at via `?ch=`), speaker or endpoint, or run an action: script pending, narrate scripted, re-narrate changed, retry or cancel jobs, auto-assign voices, pause or resume an endpoint or a book, toggle the theme, undo. Enter with nothing highlighted runs the first match.
- **Keyboard**: `?` lists every shortcut. In the reader: `j`/`k` move, `↵` opens the line, `e` edits its text, `p` plays it, `1–9` assign a speaker, `s` split, `m` join next, `[`/`]` shorten or lengthen the pause after, `c` toggle the cast rail, `f` focus mode, `/` find a chapter. The ledger, picker and cast lists take `j`/`k` too.
- **Responsive**: under `lg` the rail is a drawer carrying the open book's pages, since the tab row does not fit a phone; under `sm` the header shrinks to icons; stage layouts stack and tables scroll inside their cards.

**Editing**

- **Chapter peek and skip** — every picker row has a ⌕ popover with the raw text, the word count and **Skip this chapter**; a skipped chapter leaves every stage, the run totals and the audiobook.
- **Re-script** from the reader header: profile and chunk size, and how many hand-corrected lines the new script replaces (the old one, corrections and all, stays in the chapter's history); a "what changed" panel follows (speaker and direction changes, new and gone lines, click to jump). Edited lines carry `edited: true`.
- **Site text** in the reader: the **Site text** filter lists every line marked as the site's text or as a note, and every line the detector questions. Accept or Dismiss a suggestion under its line, turn a marked line back into story with **Not site text**, or change a line's type yourself. Each change says in a toast whether the line is now read or not, with Undo. Whether notes are read is the book's choice, made under **Reading** on its overview.
- **Direction** is a combobox of presets and directions already used in the book, free text allowed; "→ all <speaker>" applies it to every line of that speaker in the chapter.
- **Voice picker** (Voices and Cast): a popover with search, a gender filter, voices grouped by endpoint, how many speakers use each, and a button to hear the voice — the provider's own recording where it keeps one, otherwise the endpoint saying a sample sentence, billed once and kept on the server.
- **Stale nudge**: the reader header counts the lines whose audio is out of date and offers to re-narrate them; the Narration tab counts stale chapters.
- **Audit trail**: each rendered clip records voice, model, direction, style, type, time and cost. A ledger row's details (click it, or `i`) show them and spell out what differs from the script now, which is why a row is stale; a failure carries its HTTP status and body, and "copy clip record" copies what the clip recorded about its request.

**Undo and danger**

One rule, kept everywhere: **what can be undone happens at once and offers Undo; what cannot asks first.** Merging, renaming or deleting a speaker, removing a voice, an endpoint or a profile, skipping chapters, dictionary changes, script edits, bulk corrections and restores all act and toast with Undo, and `⌘Z`/`Ctrl+Z` outside a field undoes the latest. Removing a novel or a volume, deleting an audiobook, discarding an import and cancelling runs in flight cannot be undone, so they ask with a second click; the control's label and `title` say what will go, and the toast says it cannot be undone rather than offering a button that would lie. The full list is in [src/stores/README.md](../src/stores/README.md), and [tests/danger.test.ts](../tests/danger.test.ts) guards both sides.

## Import, contents and the library shelf

A web-novel EPUB is not all story. Between the chapters sit hiatus notices, health updates, release schedules, sponsor thanks, vote reminders and links, and an audiobook that reads them aloud is one nobody finishes. So a file is **read, reviewed and then added**.

**Import → Contents → Add.** **Add EPUB** sends the file to the server, which parses it, stores each chapter as Markdown and marks the chapters that look like notices ([importing an EPUB](backend.md#importing-an-epub)). _Read the file_ lands on `/book/:id/contents` with the book marked `importing`: off the shelf, not the open book, nothing running on it. **Add to library · 202 chapters** is the only way on, and says what it adds; **Cancel import** (or **Don't add this volume**) asks, then leaves no trace. The same page is the book's **Contents** afterwards — from the overview, the drawer and the picker's "skipped" count — so a chapter skipped on import is restored in the same place, and every change there applies at once to scripting, narration and export.

**A suggestion is a reason beside a title, not a removal.** The import attaches a `note` to a chapter that did not look like story: a verdict (`skip` — a notice through and through; `review` — story with something else around it), a kind, one line of reason (_Possible hiatus announcement_, _Mostly promotional links_) and the evidence behind it. Every chapter arrives **included**. `excluded` is the person's decision and the one flag every stage reads; a chapter looked at and kept records `kept`, so the suggestion stops asking. The row's checkbox _is_ the decision — ticked means in the audiobook — so there is no second selection to lose track of. Shift ticks a range of what is on screen and the volume header ticks a volume. The **To decide** tab is an inbox of only the flagged chapters, grouped by kind (_Sponsor thanks · 12_) as cards with the reason and the evidence, **Skip** / **Keep** on each, **Skip all 12** on the group and **Skip them all** for every suggested skip, each a toast with the count and Undo; single decisions are quiet, since the click is its own undo. The page opens on this tab while anything is undecided and on **All chapters** once nothing is; a clean book finds the inbox empty.

**Prologues, interludes and side stories are story.** Nothing is flagged for being short or oddly titled. Two kinds are flagged for a _look_ rather than a skip: a chapter whose title reads like a notice but whose text is story, and a chapter that mixes an author's note with story. The preview marks the note, gives its word count and offers **Keep chapter**; the note can be trimmed in Scripting once the chapter is scripted. Nothing is cut here.

**Reading never changes a tick.** The right-hand pane (a bottom sheet under `lg`) shows the chapter in full with its place in the book (_Vol. 2 · chapter 14 of 32 · #50 of 212_), beside both the inbox and the list, and the list keeps its scroll, filter and search. `↑↓` (or `j`/`k`) move, `space` ticks, `↵` reads, `n` jumps to the next chapter still to decide, `/` searches. The open tab, the filters — _Included · Suggested skips · Needs review · Skipped_ — and a search over title, number or reason live in the URL; a line under the list says when they hide chapters.

**Volumes are a line between two chapters.** The **Volumes** tab draws the book as one bar, a segment per volume and a handle at every boundary: drag a handle to move the boundary, double-click the bar to cut a volume there, right-click a segment or a handle for its menu (rename, cut here or in the middle, join with the volume before or after, skip or include the whole volume). Under the bar, the tools cut the whole book at once — evenly into N, every N chapters, or where a title says _Volume 2_, _Book Two_ or _Arc 3_, with the matches to tick first — and one row per volume shows its name (double-click or `F2` to rename), its chapter range with `←` `→` on the start, its words and listening time, the file the export would write, and a red ✕ that joins it with the volume before; a row opens to its chapters by number, titles blurred until clicked, since the titles of chapters still to come give the plot away. Every change writes the whole list of volume starts (`setVolumes` in [src/stores/library.ts](../src/stores/library.ts), the arithmetic in [src/lib/volumes.ts](../src/lib/volumes.ts)), so a cut, a join, a moved boundary and a rename are each one toast with Undo, and chapter numbers never move.

In the demo, the **Importing an EPUB** group opens this review on sample books built from [server/demo/seed/fixtures/imports.ts](../server/demo/seed/fixtures/imports.ts) rather than a parsed file, one per situation the review has to survive ([demo tools](demo.md#demo-tools)); a real EPUB can be added there too.

**The shelf.** Books come first; the drop target is a one-line hint that grows into a full-page target only while a file is over the window. Each card says the one next thing to do as a verb with a destination (_Narrate 9 chapters_, _Retry 2 failed chapters_, _Update the audiobook_ — `nextStepOf` in [src/views/library/shared.ts](../src/views/library/shared.ts)) and draws progress as one segmented bar (narrated · scripted · not started · skipped); the counts behind it appear only while the pointer or keyboard focus is on the bar. It shows what is running or has failed with a link to the Queue, and names the audiobook built from the book — **Audiobook v2 · 6 min** — with what has changed since, such as _1 chapter not in it yet_ or _2 chapters re-narrated since_ (`updateReason`). A book still in its contents review is a strip above the shelf with **Resume review** and **Discard**. The `⋯` menu ([src/views/library/BookMenu.vue](../src/views/library/BookMenu.vue)), on covers and table rows, holds Overview, Contents, Cast, Add a volume and Remove from library, which asks with a second click and names what goes. Arrow keys move between covers.

The two icons beside **Add EPUB** switch between the grid of covers and a table ([src/views/library/ShelfTable.vue](../src/views/library/ShelfTable.vue)) with one row per book and the pipeline as columns, progress per stage the way the overview shows it. The shelf has a search (title or author, every word, accents ignored, `/` to focus), filter chips with counts (**Needs attention**, **Running**, **Behind**, **Up to date**) and an order (recently added, title, author, most to do, least scripted, least narrated; the table's column headers order it too). Search, filter, order and layout sit in the URL (`?q=harbour&filter=attention&sort=todo&view=list`), so a narrowed shelf can be linked and survives a reload, and the layout is remembered for the next visit. A search that matches nothing says so and offers **Show all books**, so a stale filter is never mistaken for an empty library. In the demo, **A full shelf** adds eighteen books ([server/demo/seed/fixtures/shelf.ts](../server/demo/seed/fixtures/shelf.ts)) at every point in the pipeline to try these against.

[tests/library.test.ts](../tests/library.test.ts) covers a card's next step and the filter and order logic in [src/views/library/shelf.ts](../src/views/library/shelf.ts); [tests/volumes.test.ts](../tests/volumes.test.ts) covers the volume arithmetic — cut, join, a move clamped at its neighbours, every/evenly, titles — and [tests/contents.test.ts](../tests/contents.test.ts) covers the review's counts, every import sample's shape, import → confirm → cancel for a book and a volume, batch skip and its Undo, and a skipped chapter leaving every stage and coming back when restored.
